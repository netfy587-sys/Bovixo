Creating a Magical Atmosphere

Some sights fill us with wonder, and understanding them makes the wonder greater.

The Milky Way

Our home galaxy contains hundreds of billions of stars. From a dark place it appears as a pale band across the sky.

Meteor showers

Shooting stars are tiny bits of dust and rock burning up as they enter Earth's atmosphere at high speed.

Total solar eclipses

When the Moon passes exactly between the Sun and Earth, it can cover the Sun's bright disc completely for a few minutes.

How to start stargazing

New chapter of luck begins now, and so does careful preparation. Four steps to begin.

Find a dark place

Move away from street lights and bright buildings. Darker skies show many more stars.

Let your eyes adjust

Give your eyes about twenty to thirty minutes to adapt to the dark, and use a dim red light if you need to read a map.

Learn a few constellations

Start with easy patterns such as the Big Dipper or Orion, using a printed or app-based star chart.

Try binoculars

Even a simple pair shows the Moon's craters and the four largest moons of Jupiter.

Article · 6 min

How the Sun shines

The Sun is powered by nuclear fusion. In its core, extreme heat and pressure force hydrogen atoms together to form helium, and a small amount of mass is turned into energy in the process.

The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old. Its light takes roughly eight minutes and twenty seconds to travel the 150 million kilometres to Earth.

Article · 5 min

Why the Moon has phases

The Moon does not make its own light. We see the part of it that is lit by the Sun, and as the Moon orbits Earth, the angle between the Sun, the Moon and us changes. That changing view is what we call phases.

A full cycle from one new moon to the next takes about 29.5 days. Phases are not caused by Earth's shadow; that happens only during a lunar eclipse.

Article · 5 min

The hottest planet is not the closest

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun, yet Venus is hotter. Its thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, keeping the surface at around 465 degrees Celsius.

Our Solar System has eight planets, and Jupiter is the largest of them.

Article · 5 min

What is a light-year?

A light-year is a measure of distance, not time. It is the distance light travels in one year, which is about 9.46 trillion kilometres.

The nearest star to the Sun, Proxima Centauri, is about 4.2 light-years away. That means the light we see from it left the star a little over four years ago.

Article · 5 min

Safe & Secure: watching the sky

Never look directly at the Sun, not even during a partial eclipse, unless you use eyewear made for solar viewing that meets the ISO 12312-2 standard. Ordinary sunglasses are not enough.

For night observing, dress warmly, tell someone where you are going and take a small torch with a red light.

Astronomy terms, explained

Words you will meet often, in simple language.

Orbit
The curved path of one object around another, held by gravity.
Light-year
The distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres.
Constellation
A pattern of stars that people have named since ancient times.
Galaxy
A huge system of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity.
Exoplanet
A planet that orbits a star other than the Sun.
Eclipse
An event in which one object blocks light from another or passes into its shadow.
